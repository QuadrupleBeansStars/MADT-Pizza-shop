1 · Why build a warehouse? Ask one question

The owner asks: what was our net revenue by channel on Friday? Net means what the shop keeps after the delivery app's commission. We answered it twice: once straight from the POS and the Grab export, once from the warehouse.

From the sources: 30 lines of SQL

The query has to carry six rules by itself:

  1. Union two different shapes (POS tables, Grab CSV)
  2. Convert Grab's UTC to Bangkok time
  3. Business day: an order before 10:00 belongs to yesterday
  4. Grab repeats commission on every item row: MAX, not SUM
  5. Extra-cheese prices sit inside a text column, so it has to be parsed
  6. The POS says QR, everyone else says QR_DINEIN

From the warehouse: 7 lines

SELECT c.channel_code,
       SUM(o.net_revenue_thb)
FROM dw.fct_order o
JOIN dw.dim_channel c USING (channel_sk)
JOIN dw.dim_date d    USING (date_sk)
WHERE d.calendar_date = '2026-09-04'
GROUP BY ALL

No rules in it at all. They were all applied once, when the data was loaded.

Both queries return this table. The difference is that the source version only works if every analyst, every dashboard and the forecast model remember all six rules and write them the same way. Forget rule 4 and Grab's commission doubles. Forget rule 3 and the 01:20 order moves to Saturday.

The seventh problem: history is gone
The owner raised Pepperoni L from 329 to 349 THB on Saturday, and the POS overwrote the price. Its menu table now says 349 and nothing else. "What was margin before the price rise?" can never be answered from the POS again. An operational system answers what is true now. Analysis needs what was true then.

  • Used by the owner, analyst, forecast model

3 · How we designed it: four steps, in order

StepQuestionPizza shop answer for FCT_ORDER_LINE
1 · ProcessWhat business event are we measuring?A customer orders a menu item
2 · GrainWhat does one row mean, exactly?One menu item on one order
3 · DimensionsWho, what, when, where, how?Date, channel, customer (through the order), menu item
4 · FactsWhat numbers, true at that grain?quantity, unit_price_thb, line_net_thb

The order matters: grain before columns. If you start by listing the columns you want, you end up putting order-level money (commission, delivery fee) next to line-level money (item price) in one table, and every sum after that is wrong. Section 7 shows by how much.

Each row is a business process and becomes one or more fact tables. Each column is a dimension built once and shared by every process that uses it. Those are conformed dimensions, and they are what turn separate fact tables into one warehouse:

  • Date is shared by all nine. One DIM_DATE on the business day, so sales, stock, shifts and purchases line up by day without anyone redefining "Friday".
  • Menu item links Sell to Move stock. Units sold × BRG_RECIPE = grams of cheese used. That is the reorder loop.
  • Staff links Deliver, Count and Work. "Who was on shift when tickets ran late" is a join, not a project.

6 · Three dimension decisions

Surrogate keys, not the source's ID

menu_item_sk is an integer the warehouse assigns. PEP-L cannot be the primary key because Pepperoni L needs two rows (329 and 349). Source IDs also collide across systems, and the warehouse must not break when a source renames or renumbers something.

SCD Type 2 where history matters

A price change adds a row instead of overwriting one. Friday's sales point at key 2, Saturday's at key 6.

Star, not snowflake

category and size_code sit directly on DIM_MENU_ITEM. One join from a fact to its context, and the dimension reads like the menu. DIM_STAFF → DIM_ROLE is our one deliberate snowflake, because a role has flags of its own.

8 · Five rules for designing a fact table

Each rule is a decision already in our README. The notebook breaks each one on purpose to show what it protects.

RuleBreak it and…Where we applied it
1 · One grain per table Join orders to lines and sum commission: Grab's Friday commission reads 403.5 instead of 257.1, because GF-201 has two lines. FCT_ORDER vs FCT_ORDER_LINE; basket discounts are allocated down to lines (pro_rata), not copied
2 · Snapshot what can change Friday's Pepperoni L revenue: paid 1,037. At today's menu price: 1,047, so the price rise rewrites Friday. At Friday's list price (SCD2): 987, and the 50 THB gap is the Grab price uplift. unit_price_thb, price_delta_thb, unit_cost_thb, ot_multiplier_applied
3 · A list becomes a table Leave modifiers as text and extra cheese never counts as usage. Friday's mozzarella variance reads −100 g instead of −20 g, five times worse, and the manager looks for a thief. FCT_ORDER_LINE_MODIFIER + BRG_MODIFIER_RECIPE; BRG_SUPPLIER_DELIVERY_DAY
4 · Know what adds up Sum the daily cheese counts for Thu to Sat: "we have 16.3 kg". The fridge holds 5.2 kg. AVG(unit_price) gives 357.00; the true average is 361.86. Signed qty_delta (additive) vs qty_counted (a level); regular_hours and ot_hours stored apart
5 · One FK per source One shared source_ref_sk joins a 2 kg cheese delivery to order P-1001's pizza. No error, just a wrong row that looks plausible. Four explicit FKs on FCT_INVENTORY_MOVEMENT, with a CHECK that at most one is set
Rule 2 in one line
A snapshot answers what happened; SCD2 answers what was true. We keep both on purpose.

9 · Four kinds of fact table, and which ones we use

TypeOne row is…Rows change?Ours
Transactionone event, when it happenedNeverFCT_ORDER_LINE, FCT_PAYMENT, FCT_ORDER_EVENT, FCT_INVENTORY_MOVEMENT, FCT_PURCHASE_ORDER_LINE, FCT_SHIFT
Periodic snapshotthe state of something, every periodNever; one new row per periodFCT_INVENTORY_COUNT (daily close)
Accumulating snapshotone process, one column per milestoneYes, as milestones happenmart_delivery_sla, built from FCT_ORDER_EVENT
Factlessthat something happened or was possibleRarelyBRG_CHANNEL_MENU_ITEM as a coverage table
  • We store the event log and build the accumulating snapshot from it. A new milestone (Grab adds driver_arrived) is a new value in a row, not a new column, and the log never needs updating.
  • Stock on hand is derived from movements. A running sum of qty_delta gives the daily snapshot. The physical count is the check, and the gap between them is the waste KPI.
  • Coverage finds what didn't happen. Listed minus sold: Pepperoni M got no Grab orders, and Hawaiian sells by phone but never at the table. No sales fact can show you that.

11 · Worked example: designing fct_forecast

The README mentions a forecast table but never designs it. The four steps, applied to something new:

  1. Process. The model predicts demand. A prediction is an event: it happened at a time and has a result.
  2. Grain. One forecast of one menu item, for one business day, on one channel, from one model run. The run belongs in the grain: a Monday forecast for Saturday and a Friday forecast for Saturday are different facts.
  3. Dimensions. Menu item, date (the day forecast), channel: all three already exist for sales. Because they are conformed, forecast joins straight to actual.
  4. Facts. forecast_units. Additive across items, days and channels, but not across runs. Summing two runs for one Saturday gives 7 pizzas; the latest run says 5.

12 · Summary

Why

  • Integrate five sources into one shape
  • Define business rules once
  • Keep history the POS overwrites
  • Shape data for questions
  • Keep heavy analysis off the POS

How

  • Process → grain → dimensions → facts
  • Bus matrix of conformed dimensions
  • Surrogate keys and SCD2
  • Load facts by swapping business keys for surrogate keys, behind a quality gate
Fact table design in one line
Say the grain in one sentence, never mix two grains, snapshot what can change, turn lists into tables, store only what adds up, and give every source its own key.
