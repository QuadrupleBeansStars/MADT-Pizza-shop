Data platform design — single-location pizza shop, Bangkok

Made to order,
measured to order.

A source-to-dashboard data pipeline and warehouse model for an owner-operated pizza shop selling across dine-in QR, phone, and four delivery aggregators. Built around one question the manager actually asks every morning: what will I sell today, and what do I need to have in the fridge for it?

Persona · store manager, 1 location Channels · QR dine-in, Grab, LINE MAN, ShopeeFood, Robinhood Forecast grain · item × day × channel Timezone · Asia/Bangkok

01 Scope and assumptions

One store, so there is no dim_store — location is a constant, not a dimension. Everything below is modelled at the grain a single manager can act on: a day, a menu item, a channel, an ingredient, a shift.

  • Forecast grain is menu item × business day × channel. This is the load-bearing assumption: it decides that the sales mart is a daily item-level table, not a revenue-only table, and it is what makes ingredient forecasting possible downstream.
  • Business day ≠ calendar day. The shop trades past midnight, so a business day runs 10:00–02:00 Asia/Bangkok. Every fact carries both order_ts and the business-day date_key.
  • Aggregator orders are anonymous. Grab, LINE MAN, ShopeeFood and Robinhood do not hand over customer identity. Membership and segmentation are therefore own-channel only — see decision 04.
  • Stock is not "order minus 10%". Consumption is derived from a recipe, then reconciled against physical counts. The 10% is the variance you measure, not a rule you hard-code — see decision 02.

Why this matters for the pipeline

POS is streaming-ish and trustworthy; aggregator data is late, batched, schema-drifting, and arrives once per platform with four different column names for "order total". Everything downstream of staging must not care which of the five it came from — that is the whole job of the conform layer.

03 Pipeline architecture

A four-layer medallion pipeline. The layers are a sequence, so they are numbered; each one has exactly one contract with the next.

Layer contracts

LayerGuaranteeDeliberately not done here
1 · RawNothing is ever lost or edited. Every row keeps source_system, ingest_ts, source_file, raw payload.No typing, no dedup, no joins.
2 · StagingOne row per real-world event. Types cast, timestamps in Asia/Bangkok, channel and item names conformed, duplicates removed.No business aggregation, no KPI logic.
3 · CoreStar schema at atomic grain. Every fact has a declared grain and only surrogate keys; no NULL keys (special-case rows). History preserved via SCD2.No presentation shaping, no channel-specific hacks.
4 · AggregatesOne table per question, pre-aggregated, serving one data mart (subject area).No re-derivation of business rules — those live in core.

Architecturally this is a Kimball dimensional data warehouse: one integrated set of stars on conformed dimensions, where a data mart is a subject area within the warehouse. Section 11 lists the dimensional conventions every table follows.

04 ER model — sales and orders

Grain declarations, stated because they are the thing that makes or breaks a star schema:

  • FCT_ORDER — one row per order (any channel).
  • FCT_ORDER_LINE — one row per menu item per order.
  • FCT_ORDER_EVENT — one row per status transition per order.
  • FCT_PAYMENT — one row per tender per order (split payments are normal).
  • FCT_ORDER_LINE_MODIFIER — one row per modifier per order line.

Every fact carries its own keys to date, channel, customer and the rest; facts never point at each other. order_number and line_number are degenerate dimensions, and two facts are compared by drilling across them (section 11). Payments, order events and basket promotions are in figure 6.

Modifiers are rows, not JSON

FCT_ORDER_LINE used to carry modifiers_json — a string holding "extra cheese, no chili". That is a repeating group hidden inside a column, so no query can total extra-cheese sales without string matching. But the expensive part is not reporting, it is inventory.

Theoretical usage is derived as FCT_ORDER_LINE × BRG_RECIPE, and BRG_RECIPE explodes a menu item. Extra cheese is roughly 40 g of mozzarella that no recipe knows about — physically consumed, never counted as usage, and therefore landing in variance as unexplained shrinkage. A JSON blob quietly corrupts the exact number this model exists to produce.

  • DIM_MODIFIER — the catalogue, with modifier_type (add / remove / substitute).
  • FCT_ORDER_LINE_MODIFIER — grain: order line × modifier, with price_delta_thb snapshotted.
  • BRG_MODIFIER_RECIPE (figure 3) — what a modifier consumes, signed: extra cheese +40 g, no chili −5 g.

Theoretical usage becomes the sum of two explosions rather than one. Without the third table the first two are just tidier reporting; with it, the variance number is finally honest.

05 ER model — promotion rules

Promotions used to attach to the order only: one discount_amount_thb per order, and a promo_type label like bogo with nothing to say of what. No line knew it had been discounted, so item margin and menu engineering were computed against undiscounted revenue — wrong for exactly the items being promoted.

The fix separates the promotion's definition (what qualifies, what you get — stored as rows in BRG_PROMOTION_CONDITION and BRG_PROMOTION_REWARD) from its application (FCT_ORDER_LINE_PROMOTION, grained at order line × promotion, which is the SKU-level answer).

By SKU: DIM_MENU_ITEM already carries size_code, so it is at SKU grain — targeting by SKU needs a reference to it, not a new dimension. By condition: each condition is a row — a condition_type, an operator, and either a target_key or a plain value_num. Rows sharing a condition_group are ANDed; separate groups are ORed. A new mechanic becomes rows, never a migration.

Two rules the pipeline must enforce, because the schema alone cannot. Order-level discounts (free delivery, basket percent-off) have no natural line, so staging allocates them pro-rata and stamps allocation_method. And for every order, SUM(FCT_ORDER_LINE_PROMOTION.discount) must equal SUM(FCT_ORDER_PROMOTION.discount) — a data quality test, not a hope.

06 ER model — inventory and supply

This is the half of the model that answers "what do I need to order". It works by comparing two independently-derived numbers.

Delivery days are a table, not a string

DIM_SUPPLIER.delivery_days used to hold "Mon,Thu" — a list crammed into a scalar, and it breaks something specific. The reorder suggestion respects lead_time_days and delivery_days; you cannot compute "the next delivery date after today" from a comma-separated string without parsing text in SQL, and "which suppliers deliver on Tuesday" is not answerable at all.

BRG_SUPPLIER_DELIVERY_DAY is one row per supplier per weekday, carrying order_cutoff_time — "order by 16:00 or it ships the following delivery day" moves the recommended order date by a full day, and that date is the thing the manager acts on.

Where a movement came from: dimension keys plus a typed document reference

FCT_INVENTORY_MOVEMENT.source_ref_sk began as a single key meaning "order_line_sk or po_line_sk": a polymorphic foreign key, whose overlapping key spaces let a wrong-table join return wrong rows silently. The first fix split it into four nullable foreign keys pointing at other fact tables. That still broke two chapter 5 rules: facts referencing facts, and NULL foreign keys.

  • For analysis, dimension keys. menu_item_key (theoretical usage), modifier_key (modifier usage) and supplier_key (receipts), each pointing at row 0, Not applicable, when it does not apply. Usage by item, modifier or supplier is an ordinary star query, and no key is NULL.
  • For lineage, a typed degenerate reference. source_document_number + source_line_number (an order number, a PO number or a count session; n/a for waste), with its type held in DIM_MOVEMENT_INFO.source_document_type. This is the ref_table + ref_id form suggested in the earlier review.
Why this is safe now

Chapter 5 is explicit that in an analytic database referential integrity is enforced by the ETL, so the load checks every reference against its source fact; and because no analysis query joins on it, the wrong-table join that made the original key dangerous has nothing to run on. qty_delta stays a single signed decimal — stock on hand is SUM(qty_delta) — and the guard against a receipt booked as an outflow is a CHECK tying the sign to DIM_MOVEMENT_INFO.direction.

07 ER model — people and shifts

Staff data only earns its place if it joins to something. Here it joins twice: to shifts (labour cost per hour) and to order events (who was on the pass when tickets ran late).

Overtime

hours_worked alone cannot cost a shift, because not every hour costs the same. OT is split into its own measures rather than folded into a blended rate:

  • regular_hours and ot_hours are separate columns. Labour cost % is meaningless if a 1.5× hour is counted as a 1.0× hour, and "how much OT did we burn last month" comes before any other labour question.
  • ot_multiplier_applied is snapshotted onto the fact. Same argument as unit_price_thb on the order line — policy changes must not silently rewrite what last quarter's overtime cost. DIM_STAFF holds the current default; the fact holds what was actually used.
  • ot_eligibility sits on DIM_STAFF. Salaried managers typically accrue no OT; without the flag their long days inflate the figure and hide the OT that is real.
  • OT approval sits in the DIM_SHIFT_INFO junk dimension. Approved and unapproved overtime cost the same money but mean different things — one is a staffing decision, the other is a finding.
  • Role lives on DIM_STAFF, not in its own table. A role is a natural affinity of the staff member, so it is stored on the same row (no snowflake); SCD2 keeps the history when someone moves from prep to pizzaiolo.
Caveat — grain

OT is legally a daily concept, while FCT_SHIFT is grained per shift. If someone works two shifts in one business day the threshold spans both, so the split must be computed at staff × business_date and then allocated back to shifts. The multipliers shown (1.5× weekday, 3.0× holiday) are the common Thai defaults and should be confirmed against the Labour Protection Act before anyone runs payroll off this.

08 Modelling decisions worth defending

01 Multi-channel order provenance

Every order carries channel_key plus the platform's own order_number. Dedup and idempotent reload both key on (channel_key, order_number), so re-running yesterday's Grab export cannot double-count revenue. Without this pair, reconciling four platforms against one P&L is guesswork.

02 Recipe-driven stock, not a fixed percentage

Stock does not decrease by "order ±10%". BRG_RECIPE gives grams-per-pizza, so theoretical usage is derived from order lines. FCT_INVENTORY_COUNT supplies actual on-hand. Variance = theoretical − actual, and that variance is the waste/shrinkage KPI. The 10% is an output of the system, not an input to it.

03 Price is stored twice, on purpose

FCT_ORDER_LINE.unit_price_thb is an immutable snapshot — a receipt reprinted in 2027 must show 2026's price. DIM_MENU_ITEM is SCD2 with valid_from/valid_to so you can still ask "what did margin look like before the March price rise". Snapshot answers what happened; SCD2 answers what was true.

04 Membership only exists on own channels

Aggregators do not release customer identity, so their orders point customer_key at row 0, Not applicable, rather than storing NULL. RFM segmentation is scoped to QR and phone orders and the dashboard must label it that way; inventing a customer for every order would make the segmentation story fiction.

05 Delivery timing is an event log, not columns

FCT_ORDER_EVENT records each transition, so any duration is a difference between two rows — prep time, pickup wait, total lead time — without schema changes. Coverage is honestly uneven: QR gives full kitchen granularity, aggregators typically give accepted/pickup/delivered only. The event model degrades gracefully; a fixed-column model does not.

06 Payment, promotion and commission are separate from the order

Split tenders (half PromptPay, half cash) need their own rows. Platform-funded vs shop-funded promotions land on completely different lines of the P&L. And commission_amount_thb is what turns gross revenue into the only number that matters — net revenue the shop actually banks, which differs by up to 30% between channels.

07 A promotion's definition and its effect are different tables

Conditions and rewards say what the promo is; FCT_ORDER_LINE_PROMOTION says what it did, at SKU grain. Storing conditions as rows rather than columns lets a new mechanic ship without a migration — and pushing the discount down to the line is what keeps item margin from being fiction.

08 Overtime is measured, not averaged

regular_hours and ot_hours are separate on FCT_SHIFT, and the multiplier used is snapshotted alongside them. A blended labour rate would be simpler and would hide the single most controllable cost line the manager has.

09 No list lives in a scalar, and no foreign key is polymorphic

Three fields broke this and all three were fixed: modifiers_json, DIM_SUPPLIER.delivery_days, and FCT_INVENTORY_MOVEMENT.source_ref_sk. The first two are 1NF violations that silently disable a join; the third is worse, because an overloaded key returns wrong rows instead of failing.

10 Facts carry their own keys and are compared by drilling across

FCT_ORDER_LINE holds date_key, channel_key and customer_key itself instead of reaching them through FCT_ORDER. Joining two facts through a shared parent double-counts the parent's measures; summarizing each fact first and combining on conformed dimensions does not.

11 No NULL keys, no snowflakes

Every dimension has Not applicable / Unknown / Invalid rows, so late or bad data is loaded and flagged rather than dropped, and every attribute that describes a staff member, a menu item or an ingredient sits on that one dimension row. Section 11 has the full list of conventions.

The closed loop

The forecast is the piece that makes the whole warehouse pay for itself, and it only works because of the chain of grains chosen above:

mart_sales_daily → forecast units per item × day × channel → multiply through BRG_RECIPE → forecast grams per ingredient → subtract current on-hand from FCT_INVENTORY_MOVEMENT, add safety_stock_qty, respect supplier lead_time_days and BRG_SUPPLIER_DELIVERY_DAY (including order_cutoff_time) → a purchase order the manager can approve with one tap.

Forecasts are written back as fct_forecast (grain: item × day × channel × forecast run) so predicted-vs-actual error is itself a table you can chart. A model you cannot score is a model you cannot trust.

Dashboard, top screen

  • Net revenue by channel — gross minus commission minus shop-funded promos. Almost never what the platform app tells you.
  • Today vs forecast, live, with the gap called out.
  • Items at risk — ingredients where projected demand exceeds on-hand before the next delivery day.
  • Variance watch — ingredients whose theoretical-vs-counted gap breaches its threshold.
  • Prep time p50 / p90 by hour — where the kitchen breaks, not just that it did.

11 Dimensional design conventions (MADT8102 chapter 5)

Every table in sections 4–7 follows the conventions below. They are the terms and rules from Star schema, conformance, hierarchies, and snowflakes, applied to this shop.

Architecture

This is a Kimball dimensional data warehouse (bus architecture), not an Inmon Corporate Information Factory and not stand-alone data marts. All data is organized dimensionally, at the lowest level of detail we receive; the stars may be queried directly by analysts and the forecast model; and a data mart is a subject area within the warehouse, not a separate database:

Data mart (subject area) Stars Aggregate tables (section 9)
Sales FCT_ORDER, FCT_ORDER_LINE, FCT_ORDER_LINE_MODIFIER, FCT_PAYMENT, FCT_ORDER_PROMOTION, FCT_ORDER_LINE_PROMOTION mart_sales_daily, mart_promotion_performance
Delivery FCT_ORDER_EVENT mart_delivery_sla
Customer FCT_ORDER (own channels) + the behavioral rfm_segment on DIM_CUSTOMER mart_customer_rfm
Inventory and purchasing FCT_INVENTORY_MOVEMENT, FCT_INVENTORY_COUNT, FCT_PURCHASE_ORDER(_LINE) mart_ingredient_usage_daily
Labour FCT_SHIFT mart_labor_vs_sales

The mart_* tables are aggregate tables that serve a data mart; they are named after it, but the data mart itself is the set of stars.

Keys

  • Surrogate keys end in _key (menu_item_key, date_key): integers with no meaning, assigned by the ETL. Natural keys end in _nk. Because dimensions do not depend on the natural key, they keep history the POS overwrites (SCD2 on DIM_MENU_ITEM, BRG_RECIPE, DIM_STAFF).
  • Facts hold only surrogate keys and degenerate dimensions. Every fact carries a key to each dimension it is analysed by, directly. No fact points at another fact.
  • No NULL foreign keys. Every dimension has special-case rows:
Key row_type Used when
0 Not applicable The relationship does not exist: an aggregator order has no customer, a waste movement has no supplier, a payment is not yet settled
1 Unknown The fact arrived before its dimensional context (late-arriving data), e.g. a new Grab item not yet in BRG_CHANNEL_MENU_ITEM
2 Invalid The source supplied a code that cannot be valid

Facts are loaded against Unknown or Invalid instead of being held back, so sales are never understated; an alert fires and the row is re-pointed once the dimension catches up.

Dimensions

  • Flags hold readable values, not booleans: weekend_flag = "Weekend" / "Weekday", channel_ownership = "Own channel" / "Aggregator", membership = "Member" / "Non-member", ot_eligibility = "OT eligible" / "Not OT eligible", current_flag = "Current" / "Expired".
  • Codes travel with descriptions: channel_code + channel_name, size_code + size_name.
  • Attribute hierarchies for drilling:
  • DIM_DATE: day → month → quarter → year
  • DIM_MENU_ITEM: SKU → item → category
  • DIM_CHANNEL: channel → channel type (Dine-in / Delivery)
  • DIM_INGREDIENT: ingredient → category
  • DIM_STAFF: staff → role → role area (Kitchen / Front of house / Delivery)
  • No snowflakes. Related attributes are stored together and kept redundant. DIM_ROLE was folded into DIM_STAFF (SCD2 keeps the role history), and the preferred supplier is stored on DIM_INGREDIENT by name and natural key rather than as a key to DIM_SUPPLIER. Referential integrity is enforced by the ETL.
  • Behavioral dimension. DIM_CUSTOMER.rfm_segment is computed from past orders and refreshed monthly, so any fact can be grouped by segment without a complex query.
  • Junk dimensions hold the low-cardinality codes and flags that would otherwise sit on facts: DIM_ORDER_INFO (status, cancellation), DIM_PAYMENT_INFO (tender, settlement mode), DIM_PROMO_APPLICATION (allocation method, reward line), DIM_MOVEMENT_INFO (movement type, direction, source document type), DIM_PO_INFO (PO and receipt status), DIM_SHIFT_INFO (shift type, holiday shift, OT approval).
  • Degenerate dimensions are kept only for transaction identifiers: order_number, line_number, po_number, po_line_number, count_session_id, source_document_number.
  • Dimension roles. One DIM_DATE, used in several roles through aliases: paid_date_key and settlement_date_key on FCT_PAYMENT, order_date_key and received_date_key on purchase orders. DIM_STAFF plays counted_by_staff_key on FCT_INVENTORY_COUNT.

Facts

  • Grain is declared for every fact table (sections 4–7), and facts with different timing or different grain live in different fact tables: orders, lines, modifiers, payments, events and promotions are separate processes.
  • Nonadditive measures (prices, rates, percentages) are stored alongside, or broken down into, additive parts.
  • Comparing two fact tables is done by drilling across: summarize each fact at the common conformed dimensions (or by order_number), then combine. Facts are never joined to each other directly, because a join through a common parent double-counts — Grab's Friday commission reads 403.5 instead of 257.1 in the prototype (notebooks/warehouse_design.ipynb).
